# Preallocate core space and separate activation from forging

Start with eight cells: six occupied by required core modules and two discretionary cells. Only Enter/Exit Flow is active initially; inactive core modules have no effects but reserve space, preventing gameplay purchases from later being displaced by core activations. This supersedes ADR-0003's accompanying cells and gradual core placement.

Time activates after the first session ends. Completing a timed target then opens the starter store, with expected earnings sufficient for at least one remaining core activation and a visible path toward another. Players choose activation order; core activations are cheapest, with Forge and the expander next at approximately equal cost. Saving for gameplay modules first is allowed, and no separate purchase is needed for the two initial discretionary cells.

The starter store supports onboarding and guaranteed initial access, not repeat purchases of combination copies. It offers the starter Forge, expander, synthesizer, and infusor; the expander should be affordable early and its first cell reward quick enough to demonstrate expansion. Temporary swapping of gameplay modules is expected when space is full.

Forge candidates are not restricted to previously unlocked types: inactive core types can be rolled, but owning a copy does not bypass the core activation requirement. Duplicate core copies remain compatible with exactly one deployed copy per core type. New cells from expansion are placed during upgrade mode, and the existing connected-board reshaping rule remains.

Core activation is permanent and applies to replacement copies without another payment. Before activation, copies may be replaced or combined but cannot receive power upgrades; inactive modules receive no charge or infusor benefits, produce no effects, and preserve any held charge in a frozen state.

Ordinary gameplay modules can be used immediately when obtained through forging, without a separate activation payment. Each guaranteed starter offer remains purchasable once even if a roll already supplied the type, allowing the player to choose combination material instead of a new capability.

Multiple Forges or expanders may be deployed, each consuming space and requiring charge. Combining versus deploying duplicates remains a strategic choice; stronger Forges may improve roll rarity odds as a later effect.
