# Prove the core loop on desktop first

The first playable version tests the practice–charge–upgrade loop with Enter/Exit Flow, Time, two synthesizer types, one basic infusor, Forge, and expansion rather than implementing every focus tool first. The small forge pool includes those gameplay types and implemented core types; one synthesizer adds base production and the other supplies a build-dependent multiplier, with exact effects still to be defined. Retain the eight-cell board and reserved core positions, but do not offer unfinished features for purchase; add the other focus modules after the loop feels worthwhile. This temporarily narrows the full onboarding design in ADR-0007; exact numerical balance remains unresolved.

Desktop is the first destination, with sessions continuing while the interface is out of view. After extended sleep or application closure, the player confirms the practiced interval before rewards for that interval are finalized; ordinary background window use continues uninterrupted. This does not introduce automatic stopping at the timed target. Phone support and cross-device sync are later goals, and the implementation stack remains undecided.

For the smaller playable, retain store opening after a timed completion, but offer the starter synthesizer, Forge, and expander directly. The synthesizer is the first affordable choice, with Forge and expansion next; this is prototype pacing rather than the full core-activation economy in ADR-0007.

Purchased levels unlock alongside the starter store, after the first completed timed target. Each Forge choice samples three distinct specific module types with equal weight from the seven implemented types, then independently rolls each candidate's rarity. Additive and Conditional Synthesizers may both appear because they are different types. Candidate outcomes are generated and saved when the roll is earned, revealed during upgrade mode, and preserved across reloads or delayed reveals; later odds changes affect newly earned rolls only. The specific RNG algorithm remains an implementation choice.

Evaluate the loop through a concrete sequence: complete real practice, make an understandable build improvement, then want to begin another practice to see the build run. The first meaningful charge-powered build should emerge within two or three short sessions on the first day, with real-world evaluation extending to at most roughly a day or two initially. A rising resource counter alone is not sufficient evidence that the loop works.

Visual presentation is part of the evaluation: readable hex modules, clear charge animation, satisfying production feedback, and a quiet focus view should establish a light futuristic instrument identity. Elaborate effects and detailed art are deferred, but a bare debug interface is insufficient.

The first build operates entirely offline with local persistence and export/import backup, without accounts or a backend. Accounts and cross-device sync remain important to the eventual trusted focus companion, but are outside this initial loop evaluation.

The multiplier synthesizer's condition is the number of adjacent active core modules; inactive placeholders do not count. This creates competition for positions that can also receive charge for Forge or expansion. The basic infusor increases adjacent synthesizer contributions by a percentage: additive production directly, or only the bonus above ×1 for multipliers. Its charged state strengthens that percentage, with target charged empowerment applied separately afterward.

During flow, focus controls are prominent and the grid remains visible with calm pulsing and progress rings for chargeable modules. Reward popups and upgrade prompts are absent; detailed production breakdowns, available forge choices, and comparison tools belong in upgrade mode.

Balance introductory milestones around ten-minute sessions, targeting roughly thirty minutes of actual practice to expose the loop. Any duration and open-ended practice remain supported; ten minutes is a balancing reference, not a required rhythm.

Initially, six core modules surround one empty center cell, with a second empty cell attached outside the ring; the player may reshape the board during upgrade mode. Enter/Exit produces baseline nous but does not generate charge. Time completion is the first playable's charge source; open-ended sessions can consume banked charge but do not replenish it through Enter/Exit, an intentional tradeoff.

The basic infusor targets production capabilities, including those of core modules, rather than only dedicated synthesizers. It never improves charge generation. The test uses three provisional rarity tiers, with matching pairs combining upward and no combination beyond the highest tier. Rarity improves upgrade scaling; secondary effects are deferred from the initial test, revising the earlier one-effect-per-type proposal. The broader secondary-effect and inheritance systems remain future design work.

Use approximately one minute of Time-generated charge per ten minutes of completed planned practice as an initial tuning hypothesis, not final balance. First Forge and expansion thresholds should be very low for tutorial purposes: one ordinary timed completion's full burst should suffice for each first reward when connected. Both receivers can earn their first reward from the same burst because each eligible neighbor receives full output.

In upgrade mode, selecting a generator highlights eligible neighbors and previews queued output; selecting a receiver shows its sources and threshold progress. These previews explain the arrangement without automatically placing modules.

Each implemented module has one nous-funded upgrade level. Levels improve Enter/Exit's base nous, Time's live multiplier, the additive synthesizer's base addition, the conditional synthesizer's bonus per adjacent active core, the infusor's percentage production bonus, Forge progress per unit of charge, or expander contribution per unit of charge, respectively. Rarity controls upgrade scaling rather than adding separate upgrade tracks.

Modules start at level zero. The accepted initial primary-power factors per purchased level are ×1.20 for common, ×1.25 for uncommon, and ×1.30 for rare. Multiplier upgrades affect only the bonus above ×1; Forge and expander upgrades affect progress per charge. The first upgrade costs 10 nous, with subsequent costs growing by ×1.6 and rounded upward, using the same costs across rarities initially. Power growth and price growth are separate tuning parameters, and these values require playtesting rather than being treated as final balance.

After the initial threshold of sixty progress, forging thresholds grow by ×1.5 per earned roll globally, while expansion thresholds double per earned cell globally. Each system has a separate shared progress meter, with deployed modules contributing according to charge intake and efficiency; Forge combination has no threshold history to inherit (ADR-0009).

All three test rarities can appear from the first forge roll, with probabilities heavily weighted toward the lowest tier and only a slight chance of higher tiers, leaving room for later odds improvements. Guaranteed starter copies are lowest-tier; combination provides a predictable route upward. Exact probabilities remain to be tuned.

Launch the first playable locally in a browser, with offline operation after setup and save export/import. Its presentation should feel like a game or modular instrument rather than a conventional website; desktop packaging is deferred and the specific stack remains undecided.

Early progression should accumulate small whole-number quantities of nous, with individual units and successive powers of ten feeling meaningful. Very large numbers are appropriate later, but prestige and later progression systems are deferred until the core loop works. Next, review an explicitly provisional balance table and its predicted first three ten-minute sessions before implementing numerical progression.

The opening numerical proposal is accepted as a first playtest baseline, subject to clarifying the shared production formula: Enter/Exit contributes 0.1 nous/s, the additive synthesizer contributes 0.05 nous/s, Time multiplies by 1.2, the conditional synthesizer adds a 10% multiplier bonus per adjacent active core, and the basic infusor increases eligible contributions by 20%. Proposed charged production bonuses are 50%, approximately 67%, and 75% at received strengths 1, 2, and 3. A ten-minute timed completion awards sixty seconds at strength 1, and initial Forge and expansion thresholds are sixty charge each.

Provisional starter prices are 40 nous for the additive synthesizer, 40 for the infusor, 60 for the conditional synthesizer, and 80 each for Forge and the expander. Initial per-candidate rarity probabilities are 99% common, 0.9% uncommon, and 0.1% rare across three independently generated candidates. Display and spending use whole nous while fractional production is retained internally. These are tuning values, not final balance; the intended no-upgrade example reaches 60 nous after the first ten-minute session, 132 after the second, then buys additive plus Forge for 120 and earns approximately 113.4 during the third with appropriate charge adjacency.
