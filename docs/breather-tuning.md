# Breather production economy: rebalance record (issue #417)

The three-time-kind world the breather created — flow practice, paid rest,
upgrade quiet — re-examined as a production economy: is the boosted phase
worth taking, never worth faking, and is flow practice ever taxed for it?
The decision is ADR-0073's mechanic with every launched figure re-validated
against representative boards; **every figure stands as launched** — each
defended below with the arc evidence, none moved. Every number here is
provisional tuning, exactly as the figures it defends; a later pass that
moves one re-records this note beside it. Method follows the profile waves'
own: accepted sample niches over representative boards, run through the
authoritative arc harness (`breather-calibration.ts`, gated by
`breather-tuning.test.ts`) — real sessions, summaries, breathers, and
reserves through the engine's own actions and walks; the only fixture work
is the roster seats themselves, the shape never the wealth. The engine
suite's committed tier pins the observable bands on one seed; the battery
below is deterministic (the niche boards' economy never touches the roll
streams), so one seed is the battery.

## The arc

Every sample rides the same shape: a warmup session (steady state —
reserves banked, feats earned — never measured), the reference 25-credited-
minute session the spec's own example names, the 5-minute breather its
1:5 divisor mints, then a 30-minute next session whose first five minutes
are the opening window. Each board runs twice: breather taken, breather
declined. One-minute steps throughout; one note written per session, the
ordinary session shape.

The sample niches:

- **Naked** — the opening Sine alone. The first breather a player can ever
  take (the 10-credited-minute gate) lives here, on a board with no charge
  economy at all.
- **Starter** — the shelf trio around the Sine: Flywheel, Forge, Booster.
  The board the first real breathers ride.
- **Charged** — the Saw's home: the charge specialist fed by the Flywheel,
  the profile wave's ×3 delta niche.
- **Temporal** — the Afterglow in an Octave with the Sine, delivered to by
  the Pulsewell: the two designs the boundary pacing criterion names.
- **Responders** — every launch responder (Triangle, Square, Saw, Sideband,
  Afterglow) in one formation beside the Sine's absent mark: the
  maximum-launch-boost board.
- **Specialists** — #416's wave (Release, Standby, Tail) beside a Flywheel:
  the S-band signatures the launch spread must stay clearly under, read on
  the same arc the Responders niche rides.

## Criterion 1 — the launch spread (×1.1–×1.25, Sine absent): defended

What a breather yields per minute on each sample board, against the minting
session's mean credited ν/s:

| Board | Flow ν/s | Breather ν/min | Boosted per minted minute | Dwarf ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Naked | 0.1040 | 6.24 | 1.000 | 0.200 |
| Starter | 0.1239 | 8.99 | 1.209 | 0.242 |
| Charged | 0.2529 | 23.04 | 1.519 | 0.304 |
| Temporal | 0.2666 | 20.64 | 1.291 | 0.258 |
| Responders | 0.8127 | 56.99 | 1.169 | 0.234 |
| Specialists | 0.4916 | 42.93 | 1.455 | 0.291 |

Reading the columns against the priorities:

- **Worth taking**: every response-carrying niche's boosted minute
  out-produces its flow minute (1.17–1.52). The raise is real on every
  board shape.
- **Never dwarfing**: the whole breather stays at 20–30% of the session
  that minted it. The 1:5 divisor times any launched response is bounded
  by construction (R/5 ≤ 0.36 even at the deepened Standby's ×1.8), and
  the measurement confirms it on every niche.
- **The spread reads as authored**: the committed gate re-derives every
  response per voice on the Responder board — each voice's flow→breather
  ratio over the Sine's absent mark as control — and lands exactly on the
  authored figures: Triangle ×1.100, Square ×1.100, Saw ×1.200, Sideband
  ×1.201, Afterglow ×1.25, Sine ×1.000. The board-level figures above the
  authored spread (Charged 1.519, Temporal 1.291) are the board's own
  charge leg, not the axis: the breather runs on the reserve the session
  just banked, while the flow mean carries uncharged minutes. That
  composition is the mechanic working — paid rest spends banked charge at
  the boosted rate — and it is why the Charged niche tops the table
  without any figure moving.
- **The absent mark is exact at board level**: the warmup session has
  already earned every boost the Naked board can carry, so its breather
  minute equals its practice minute exactly — ×1.000, dwarf 0.200. The
  response component is exactly none.
- **The gate and cap bound the mint both ways**: exactly ten credited
  minutes mint the smallest allowance (2:00); a 90-minute session mints
  the 10-minute cap, whose breather yields 6.24 ν/min on the Naked board
  against the session's 6.00 — dwarf ratio 0.116. The cap is what keeps
  long sessions' breathers from scaling with them; the gate keeps short
  sessions from minting trivial ones.

## Criterion 2 — the allowance (÷5 divisor, 10-minute gate, 10-minute cap): defended

The 1:5 feel: a 25-minute session mints a 5-minute breather producing
20–30% of that session's flow-equivalent production — a real paid rest
that never replaces the practice it followed. The boosted yield per minted
minute is the table above's fourth column: 1.00–1.52 flow-minute
equivalents, the response spread plus the board's live charge leg.

The divisor is also load-bearing against the one cost the boundary has
(see criterion 3): the drain-to-bank ratio on a focus-fed board is
`10 ÷ divisor` — scale-free in the session's length. At 1:5 a focus-only
niche's breather spends twice its banked window; at 1:8 it still spends
1.25×; only divisors past 1:10 begin to preserve the charged opening, and
they do it by cutting the mechanic's worth to a dwarf ratio near 0.1 —
half the yield for a cost that is bounded anyway. The divisor stands at
the spec's own example because the alternative trades the mechanic's
honest worth for a marginal pacing gain the immediate earners already
recover (below).

## Criterion 3 — boundary pacing: validated, cost bounded and recovered

A breather drains reserves and turns forge meters at the live rate —
generators and forge carry no breather response (spec §3), and the drain
measurement pins it: the Starter niche's Flywheel banks 150 at close and
reads exactly 90 after one breather minute — the plain 1/s emission, not
a boosted one. The forge meters turned on that charge bank a roll inside
the same breather (progress +16, one crossing).

The cost lands on the next session's opening charge economy:

| Board | Reserve at open (taken / declined) | Opening 300 s (taken vs declined) | Whole next session | Whole arc (taken vs declined) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Naked | 0 / 0 | 0.0% | 0.0% | +9.1% |
| Starter | 0 / 150 | −21.6% | −4.5% | +8.6% |
| Charged | 0 / 150 | −33.1% | −8.0% | +9.6% |
| Temporal | 0 / 75 | −5.4% | −0.8% | +11.3% |
| Responders | 0 / 0 | 0.0% | 0.0% | +10.6% |
| Specialists | 0 / 150 | −9.8% | −1.8% | +12.3% |

Three facts keep this from being starvation:

1. **The cost is bounded** — the worst opening window (Charged, −33%)
   is a 5-minute read on one niche; the whole next session never loses
   more than 8.0%.
2. **The arc never goes behind** — the breather's spend produced at the
   boosted rate while it lasted, so taking the breather is worth +8.6% to
   +12.3% over declining it on every niche. The charge is re-timed, never
   taxed: the same banked window empowers the next session's opening when
   saved, and the breather's boosted production plus forge progress when
   spent.
3. **The economy refills by design** — immediate earners (notes, goals)
   credit through the next session's own play, and the focus window
   re-banks at every session end regardless of reserve state, so no arc
   ratchets downward.

The temporal reads behave as breather-spec §1 describes: the breather
advances the departed session's clock (1500 → 1800 across the 5-minute
breather), the Afterglow breathes at its peak warmth (×1.25 from the
break's open to its end — the compound with its ×1.25 response reading
×1.5625 on that one voice during the break, its temporal identity rather
than a breather signature) and re-warms from ×0.3 when the next session
opens; the Pulsewell's burst windows keep cycling through the break — the
committed read catches the break opening in the steady stretch, entering
the ×2.5 burst window thirty-five seconds in, and the reserve reading the
burst's ×2.5 drain exactly (30 steady seconds at ×0.5 plus 5 burst
seconds at ×2.5 off the 75 banked) — the cycle spending the earned
quantity proportionally faster, never more of it.

## Criterion 4 — the allocation across the breather boundary: verified

The mode join in the allocation fingerprint re-solves **exactly once** at
the boundary on every niche (memo-solve count across `startBreather` = 1,
read on the display basis), the ending mode change re-solves **at most
once** (zero is a legitimate memo hit when the inputs returned to their
pre-breather shape), and the breather's steady steps never solve beyond
the real input boundaries (reserve depletion, burst windows) — the key
timelines are piecewise-constant everywhere, never per-tick. The gate also
bounds steady solves at eight over the five-minute break on every niche
(including the re-pick niche), sampling at one-second and half-second
ticks and requiring identical solve counts at both cadences. Repeated
solves that retain the same keys therefore fail independently of key churn.

Where a differing breather optimum genuinely exists, the re-pick is
coherent. The re-pick niche (a contended C4 between a Fifth whose partner
is a leveled G4 and a Flat seventh whose partner is a charged, breathing
Afterglow) reads the Fifth in upgrade quiet and re-picks to the Flat
seventh at the join — the charged, peak-warmth, response-scaled weights
reorder the certified optimum — and the re-picked set then holds for the
whole breather: one solve at the join, piecewise-constant keys, and the
retained solve equal to a cold no-hint solve at every step of the walk.
Equal-output retention holds; nothing thrashes.

## Criterion 5 — specialist headroom: held

The Responders niche (launch spread) yields 1.169 boosted minutes per
minted minute; the Specialists niche (#416's S band) yields 1.455 on the
same arc — a 1.24× separation on the observable both waves price, with
the launch dwarf ratio (0.234) and the specialists' (0.291) sorted the
same way. The load-bearing distinction is the authored axis itself: the
launch roster tops out at ×1.25 (A band) against the S band's ×1.4–×1.8,
and the grades carry that distinction wherever the profile renders. The
Afterglow's ×1.5625 in-breather compound is its peak-warmth clock reading
— the same ×1.25 factor any long flow session rides — not a breather
figure, and it stays under the deepened Standby's authored ×1.8.

## Limitations and deferred work

- The niches are fixture-seated boards at level 0 — the shape, not the
  wealth, is what a niche prices. Progression scales every leg by the same
  power, so the ratios ride along; absolute figures do not.
- The drain-to-bank ratio is stated for the focus-only extreme; boards
  with immediate earners refill during the next session's own play, which
  is why the Temporal niche's opening barely moves.
- The battery is deterministic and seed-invariant by construction (no
  rolls fire on the niches); a future roster wave that makes the niches
  roll must re-run it across seeds.
- No figure moved, so no coefficient table changed and no pinned test was
  rewritten. The committed gate (`breather-tuning.test.ts`) pins the
  observable bands — worth taking, never dwarfing, unboosted drain,
  bounded opening, coherent re-pick, headroom — and must be re-banded
  beside the note by whichever pass moves a figure.
