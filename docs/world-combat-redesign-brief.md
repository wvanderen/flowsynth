# World combat redesign

Status: owner-confirmed design; not implemented. This brief owns the design; the linked issues own their implementation and verification scope. The [earlier World feedback brief](world-layer-feedback-brief.md) records the shipped baseline.

## Intent and priorities

Modules build power; World gives that power a reason to exist. A build can lose to a recognizable wall, and either a better setup or a stronger era can change the outcome. Combat runs automatically during flow; preparation belongs between sessions. Protect focus first, prove a consequential build choice second, and tune the long-run power fantasy third. More HP that only makes the same build wait longer is not progress.

## Confirmed direction

- **Enemy progression.** [ADR-0088](adr/0088-world-victories-last-for-the-lifetime-and-enemies-take-turns.md) owns lifetime victories and ordered pressure. Before discovery the Ledger explains actual production pressure; after discovery the World layer identifies the enemy applying it and the current penalty. In the first proof, each enemy has one fixed penalty rather than bands that deepen with current-era production. Give enemies distinct names and symbols; bespoke effects are not needed for the first proof.
- **Footprint.** [ADR-0089](adr/0089-price-cells-by-distance-without-selling-pitch-access.md) owns the shared footprint, distance-band prices, and free reshaping within the paid bands.
- **Faceplates.** World and Modules use the same hex outline; faceplate design distinguishes the layers. This replaces using a flat-crowned silhouette as the distinction. Coordinates and interaction geometry are not the presentation mechanism. [Instrument standards](instrument-standards.md) govern the rest of the presentation.
- **Combatants and gear.** The creature has its own HP, a useful base action, and can be defeated. Adjacent weapon, defense, and spell gear change what the creature can do; they are not independent turrets. One adjacent gear copy serves one creature at a time; different copies can serve the same creature without a per-type slot cap or equip fee. One adjacent creature equips automatically; a contested copy takes a bearer selection in upgrade mode and is never re-assigned automatically during a session. Space is the limit. The base attack and each equipped weapon retain their own cadence; multiple armor pieces reduce the remaining incoming damage rather than summing to immunity. In the first melee fight the creature borders the enemy; its gear need not. Ordinary weapons have a timed uncharged action and a discrete fueled effect. The Charge Blade is an exception: its blade attack requires charge, but the creature can still act without it. Spells can bank charge to a threshold. Additional supply does not directly scale per-hit damage. The first defensive item passively reduces incoming damage; the first spell automatically spends charge on a burst. No separate shield pool, healing spell, or configurable cast trigger is needed for that proof.
- **Regeneration.** A defeated creature returns to the tray, leaves its gear deployed, and cannot be placed again until fully healed. It never redeploys itself. A wounded, undefeated creature may be benched to heal and redeployed before full health. Creature and enemy HP persist through prestige; defeat is not a new full-health attempt. Wounded enemies heal even while fighting or below their current-era threshold. Only the active wall can fight, be fought, or apply a penalty. Regeneration uses the World simulation clock (flow and breathers, not pauses, upgrade mode, or offline time), with the same honesty rules. Tune for an outright win, viable attrition over several deployments, and a weak build that loses ground to enemy regeneration.
- **Charge.** The Charge Column lifts charge from Modules. A distinct World occupant transports it within World without generating it. Each conduit in the first proof has one chosen outgoing edge. Loops must not duplicate charge. Placed and connected weapons or spells can fill a bounded reserve while unequipped, but cannot spend it on combat. Routing competes with gear for space.
- **Scaling.** Player power and enemy strength should span orders of magnitude. A current opponent can beat an underprepared build; meaningful prep can reverse a loss; a stronger era can beat a wall that waiting alone could not solve. Exact curves remain tuning.
- **Acquisition.** Credited practice is the primary gear source. Blacksmith, selectable at permanent World discovery once implemented, owns a weapon forge meter for the selected habit–class pair. A completed meter earns a deliberate choice from a small, known weapon selection. Armor, spells, and creatures do not enter that first meter. When that path ships, fresh ordinary module drafts use their World slot for Columns and conduits, not weapons. Special module drafts, previously generated offers, and owned copies stay intact. A repeatable board-powered combat-gear source is deferred unless it solves a demonstrated gap. At live release, permanent World discovery grants the full starter kit unplaced, once. Existing discovered saves get the new starter pieces once, without duplicating previous Column or Charge Blade grants.

## First proof

Start with a playable fixed-kit prototype: one creature, the existing Charge Blade, one defensive item, one spell, a Charge Column, and a World charge route. Prove one named enemy fights back and that the same player can lose, change something consequential about their setup, and win. Then prove a second wall that rewards a stronger era by spending banked Arete on Hone, the existing persistent, type-wide Charge Blade upgrade, not by gaining hidden stats for resetting. Prefer this proof to widening the upgrade tree or rewriting the claim curve. The live kit and migration, ordinary weapon pattern, and Blacksmith are follow-on slices. The proof does not require the full class roster, randomized gear, bespoke enemy effects, or a change to live saves.
