# Retro 2026-10-10: the intent-drift class

Findings from the review of the instrument-refit iteration (#268–#280). Feeds the standards amendment sessions; briefs for the fixes hang off the decisions recorded there.

## Diagnosis

The failure class is **letter-passes, spirit-fails**: implementers and reviewers act on the written artifact, and wherever the artifact is silent the LLM default (verbose, defensive, system-speak) fills the gap. Three mechanisms compounded it:

1. **Lossy compression at the artifact boundary.** Spoken intent ("if we need detailed information, it can pop up") was recorded as mechanism ("deeper mechanics live in the tooltip layer", [instrument-standards.md](instrument-standards.md)) with no doctrine for tooltip existence or copy. The context of that round was killing `<details>` boxes, so the standard reads as *where text goes*, and the sample review (line 63) actively teaches "moves to the tooltip layer".
2. **Review judges surfaces, never tooltip content.** The review criteria cannot fail a paragraph in a tooltip, a card outline around one, or a redundant tooltip repeating the text beside it. The fossil: `render.ts` Harmonic capacity row — `<small>+1 whole chord per voice</small>` directly beside a tooltip restating it as a sentence.
3. **Parallel streams turn drift into precedent.** Thirteen issues ran in separate worktrees at once; each implementer reads merged main as "the codebase style" and converges on it. One drifted surface became the template; corrections only arrived post-merge.

This is the **second** appearance of the class: #254's problem statement records "repeated user steering toward compact readouts and the alien instrument identity" from the previous era. The #243 standards closed the surface-paragraph and nested-card channels; the slop moved to tooltips, rounded controls, and system-speak names.

Confirmed gaps in [instrument-standards.md](instrument-standards.md):

- No **tooltip doctrine**: when a tooltip exists, what its copy is, that redundancy is deleted rather than moved.
- No **naming register**: purchases are named with mechanics terms ("First ceiling") because no doc assigns the job of naming products in the fantasy register. CONTEXT.md's `_Avoid:_` pattern exists for module names but is not extended to purchases.
- No **control inventory**: the card ban (line 21) is scoped to panel furniture, so rounded legacy buttons are technically legal. Bar orientation, goal palette, and co-listing order (mutators lead) are unpinned — mutators-above-modules lives only in the hex detail's implementation.
- The pinned prototype demonstrates a catalog readout, not controls.

Briefs that enumerate **elements** instead of jobs, priorities, non-goals, and one-place rules invite furniture: the PLAN face renders every listed element plus supportive extras (planned-practice section, custom-duration section, time shown in three places). Contrast: "no duplicated balance fills a header" — a one-place rule — worked wherever written.

## Decisions the user has already made (recorded 2026-10-10)

- Tooltips are the disclosure layer for detail, not a content destination; redundant information is cut, not relocated.
- Catalog entries get proper-noun product names in the instrument's fantasy register (the "Silicon Chordorators" shape: evocative name + one terse effect line + hoverable terms), never mechanics terms as names.
- Domain terms in copy are themselves hoverable ("Harmonic capacity", "memorized") — term-hover replaces description prose.
- Catalog groups by what is being purchased (semi-permanent upgrades for modules); show-don't-tell with a blank-module preview instead of descriptions; ladder rungs collapse into single entries where they are one product.
- The planned/set time appears in one place in the Focus sheet; the whole time-set UI is rethought as simple compact controls.
- Goal progress bars are vertical, a couple more fit, and each goal takes a user-chosen palette color.
- Wherever modules and mutators co-list, mutators lead (canonical, matching the hex detail).
- Rounded legacy button style retires; the refit needs to come together as one design system.

## Candidates

1. Write the tooltip doctrine + review criteria into the standards (existence rule, one-line copy ceiling, deletion over relocation, no card outline).
2. Add the voice/naming standard (register, canonical examples, `_Avoid:_` for purchases).
3. Briefs carry intent, priorities, non-goals, and one-place rules — not element lists.
4. Break the precedent loop: standards-conformance sweep of merged surfaces before the next wave lands.
5. Extend the pinned prototype in a new approval round: controls sheet (buttons, switches, bars, chips, tooltip styling, goal palette) + co-listing order.

## Resolution — 2026-10-10

Settled in the grill session the same day. Candidates 1–3 amended [instrument-standards.md](instrument-standards.md) (disclosure doctrine, hoverable terms, naming register, co-listing order, review criteria) and created [briefs.md](agents/briefs.md) with its AGENTS.md pointer. Product names settled: **Poly Expander** (nous capacity rungs), **Expansion Bays** (capacity ceilings, one entry bought twice at rising price), **Rack Extenders** (octave rows, one entry bought twice at rising price), **Chord Catalysts** (capacity discounts, same shape), **Headroom** (replaces Horizon break). Focus decisions: the console clock is the single planned-duration readout; switch re-click while PLAN is open starts flow; habit selection becomes a one-tap chip row; habit/goals door tiles retire (Notes stays); goal bars go vertical (~5 + overflow) with a six-token palette chosen at goal creation. Candidate 4 (conformance sweeps) rides wave boundaries and #281; candidate 5 (controls-sheet prototype: button geometry, vertical bar form, tooltip styling, palette swatches) remains open — prototype, approval, re-pin.
