Opening learning arc: reaching the first chord through play

The board model is settled: an octave-stack lattice where pitch lives in the cell and any two adjacent synths almost fall into a chord. So the opening question is no longer can a new player form a chord — it is whether they notice, attribute, and want more. Three pedagogies, same accepted interaction language (click to place, drag to move with live swap, double-click returns a synth to the tray, click a synth for its bloom), same placeholder economy (0.1 ν/s per synth, geometric upgrade, 30 ν cells, one-time row gate upward).

Decide while playing: which pedagogy opens the game — a silent board (A), staged wins (B), or a live-in chord to perturb (C)? Each implies an opening grant (#122 deferred the count here): A grants 3 free synths onto an empty triangle; B pre-places a fifth and grants 2 more; C pre-places the whole power chord and grants 2 plus a ready cell. Is the first session the arc's finale or should practice start earlier? Does any of it need words at all? Success test: placed, chorded, perceived, rearranged — inside minutes, with no Carrier lesson.
