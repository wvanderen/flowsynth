Isomorphic layout: which interval mapping for the hex board?

Four playable pitch laws for the carrierless lattice decided in #117. Same interactions everywhere: click to place, drag to move, double-click to remove, sound optional. A hex board has three neighbour directions but only two independent intervals — the third is always their difference — so every mapping spends a fixed budget: watch what becomes a tight shape, and what leaves the board. Numbers are placeholders.

Decide while playing: what “key” means on the chosen lattice (a global tuning? nothing?), whether the octave is playable, and what the cell economy can still gate once pitch stops being bought with depth — the production ticket hangs on this.
